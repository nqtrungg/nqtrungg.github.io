The problem

Where are default, loss and outstanding exposure concentrated - and how should portfolio teams respond?

Consumer-lending portfolios contain millions of loans with different prices, borrower profiles, purposes, terms and outcomes. Managers need consistent definitions for exposure, default, loss and predicted risk without presenting the model as an automated lending decision engine.

  • Which active loans combine elevated predicted PD with meaningful outstanding principal?
  • How do grade, purpose, term, FICO and DTI change risk concentration?
  • How should underwriting, pricing, monitoring and risk appetite respond?

Dataset

LendingClub Accepted Loans 2007-2018 Q4. The cleaned analytical population contains one record per accepted or funded loan; terminal outcomes are isolated for supervised modeling to reduce target censoring.

Model / analysis

Interpretable Logistic Regression

The pipeline uses balanced class weights and preprocessing fitted within training. The threshold is selected on 2016 validation data and locked for the 2017 test cohort. The model supports ranking, segmentation and watchlists - not standalone adverse decisioning.

Test ROC-AUC0.6989
Average Precision0.3908
Recall0.7062
Brier Score0.1652

Key insights

Portfolio risk is elevated.

19.98% of terminal loans defaulted and net credit loss reached $2.68B.

Exposure changes priority.

High PD should be combined with outstanding principal and delinquency rather than used alone.

Term and segment matter.

Grade, purpose, term, FICO and DTI reveal materially different risk concentrations.

Model performance is moderate.

The model captures about 70.6% of defaults but produces substantial false positives.

Recommendations

  • Use a Balanced risk-appetite scenario as a backtesting baseline, not a production policy.
  • Strengthen review for weak-FICO/high-DTI combinations and selected high-risk 60-month segments.
  • Price for expected loss plus funding, operating, capital and target-margin requirements.
  • Route high-PD, high-exposure active loans to manual monitoring rather than automated collections action.

Limitations

  • Accepted-loan data excludes rejected applicants, preventing approval-policy evaluation.
  • Historical 2007-2018 outcomes may not represent current borrowers or macro conditions.
  • No monthly servicing snapshots means true roll rates and cure rates cannot be measured.
  • Fair-lending review, drift monitoring and champion-challenger governance remain required.
